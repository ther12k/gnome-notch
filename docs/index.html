The top of your screen, alive.

Notch is a small, dark droplet that hangs from the top-center of your GNOME desktop. It springs open when you hover, keeps your music, focus timer, AI quotas, battery and water one glance away — and blinks back when everything is calm.

FAQ

Good questions.

Which GNOME Shell versions are supported?

GNOME Shell 46 (Ubuntu 24.04 and friends). It uses standard extension APIs — PanelMenu chrome, MPRIS, GLib timers — with no hacks into the shell.

Do I need Python?

Only for the AI-quota module: a small local ai-collector.py reads your usage. Everything else (music, focus, battery, water, eyes) is pure extension code with no dependencies.

Why do I need to log out once after installing?

GNOME Shell only scans the extensions directory at login, so a brand-new extension UUID isn't visible until the next sign-in. Once — then every later ./deploy.sh hot-reloads without logging out.

How do I configure it?

Click the pill → toggle modules in 🧩 Modules, pick the pill style, or open ~/.config/gnome-notch/config.json for focus durations, water goal, poll interval and thresholds — then ↻ Reload settings in the menu.

Where does the AI quota data come from?

From your own accounts: the collector reads the same config as aiacctool (~/.config/ai-usage-monitor/) and runs entirely on your machine. No data is sent anywhere.
